The problem

The literature search is where theses stall.

Forty tabs, no plan

You search a bit everywhere, find something, lose it again, and can't say later how you found what you found.

References that don't exist

Chatbots can produce citations that look real but lead nowhere. One fake source in your reference list is one too many.

Which database, which words?

PubMed, Scopus, PsycINFO, Google Scholar: each has its own syntax. Synonyms, MeSH terms and Boolean operators are easy to get wrong.

Paste the prompt, fill in your topic, field and level. The AI works through one phase per answer, ends with a checklist and waits until you are ready for the next one.

# ROLE
You are an expert academic librarian and research-methods coach. Do only Phase 0 of a literature search: turn my topic into a precise, searchable research question.

# INPUTS
- Research topic: [RESEARCH_TOPIC]
- Purpose and level: [PURPOSE]  (e.g., bachelor's thesis, master's thesis, seminar paper)
- Field of study: [FIELD_OF_STUDY]
- Criteria, optional: [CRITERIA]  (default: last 10 years, English, peer-reviewed)

# RULES
1. No invented sources or thesaurus IDs. If you have web access, check every controlled-vocabulary term in the official thesaurus (e.g., MeSH, APA Thesaurus, ERIC) and give the ID as proof. Otherwise mark it "(verify)".
2. Look up each core term AND its main synonyms; the preferred heading is often a synonym.
3. Short sentences a first-year student can follow. Plain Markdown tables.

# DELIVER
- A framework that fits the field (PICO/PECO for health, PEO for qualitative work, SPIDER for mixed methods, concept-based otherwise), as a table, with one sentence on the choice.
- The topic as one precise, searchable question.
- Concept table: each core concept with synonyms, related terms, spelling variants and controlled vocabulary.
- Vocabulary pitfalls (new descriptors, ambiguous terms that bring noise).
- Include/exclude table: time, language, publication type, population, intervention/exposure, outcome, design.
- Numbered open decisions, each with your suggestion, and a sample reply (e.g., "1 OK, 2 only RCTs").
- End with: "Phase 0 done. Phases 1-6 (access, Zotero, tested search strings, import, citing in Word) are in the full prompt at litreviewcoach.com."

FAQ

Questions before you buy.

Is this allowed at my university?

That depends on your institution's rules on AI use. The prompt reminds you to check them and to disclose AI use where required. It never queries licensed databases for you and never downloads paywalled PDFs.

Does it work without university access?

Yes. Enter "none" as your university and the prompt switches to open databases and legal routes such as open access, public libraries and interlibrary loan.

Which citation styles are supported?

APA 7th Edition by default. You can set any other style, for example Harvard, Chicago, MLA or IEEE, and Zotero formats your reference list in it.

Does it work in ChatGPT and Claude?

Yes. It is one prompt, tested in both Claude and ChatGPT. It follows the same 7 phases and rules in both; in ChatGPT the answers per phase come out a little shorter. Turn on web search so the AI can check thesaurus terms and test search strings.

Do I need Zotero experience?

No. Phase 2 walks you through installing and setting up Zotero with exact menu paths.

Does the AI download paywalled PDFs for me?

No. You search licensed databases yourself. The prompt never suggests shadow libraries and points you to legal ways in: open-access versions, interlibrary loan or emailing the author.

Will it write my thesis?

No. It plans and documents your literature search. Reading, judging and writing stay with you.
